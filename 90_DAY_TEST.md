# The 90-Day Test

**Question this test answers:** Will independent financial advisors and estate attorneys pay real money for VerbaPost as an heir-acquisition / client-retention letter service?

**Window:** July 6, 2026 → October 4, 2026 (90 days)
**Decision date:** October 5, 2026 — no extensions except as defined in the Decision Rules below.

---

## The Offer

**Positioning:** *The heir-acquisition letter service for independent advisors.*
Every letter puts the advisor's name, on linen paper with a real stamp, into the mailbox of the person about to inherit the money — at the moment of maximum emotional relevance. Retention and acquisition are the same mechanism: the heir is the next client.

**Product:** $99 per client engagement. Includes: AI phone interview with the client (senior), transcription and editing, physical letters mailed to each heir (multi-recipient — the differentiator no competitor has), QR code to the recorded voice, firm branding on the letter.

**Pilot lever (optional):** Up to 3 free pilot engagements total across all prospects — one favorite client each, full service, in exchange for a debrief call and, if the family reacts well, a testimonial and conversion to paid.

## The Segment (updated Jul 7 — founder's call: CFPs are the customer)

**PRIMARY:** Independent CFP® professionals — solo and small fee-only/RIA practices (Nashville metro: Brentwood, Franklin, Green Hills). They own their marketing budget, live on long-term client relationships, and feel heir-attrition risk directly in AUM. The website, pitch, and demo all speak their language.

**SECONDARY:** Estate planning attorneys — approach as referral partners and EPCMT warm doors rather than primary buyers.

**NO:** Captive/branded advisors (Edward Jones, Merrill, etc.) — compliance-blocked, no discretion. Already tested; do not spend more cycles here.

## The Protocol

1. **List:** 30 named targets (25 independent CFPs/RIAs, 5 estate attorneys as referral doors), Nashville metro. Seed names in TARGET_LIST_DRAFT.md; fill the rest from napfa.org and feeonlynetwork.com directories.
2. **The artifact IS the pitch:** each target receives a real VerbaPost letter — linen, typewriter font, real stamp, addressed to them personally, QR to a sample story.
3. **The follow-up call — mandatory.** 3–5 business days after the letter lands. No exceptions. (The 25-letter campaign failed the last time only because this step was skipped; letters without calls are half a test.)
4. **The ask:** a 20-minute meeting → pilot or paid engagement. Track every response verbatim, especially objections.
5. **EPCMT path:** land one coffee with an Estate Planning Council of Middle Tennessee member by mid-August; attend the September 14 dinner as a guest.
6. **Cadence:** minimum 2 completed conversations per week. A conversation = a real dialogue with a decision-maker, not a voicemail.

## Minimum Valid Effort (or the test is VOID, not failed)

- ≥ 20 completed conversations with in-segment decision-makers
- ≥ 20 sample letters mailed, each followed by a call
- EPCMT contact attempted (coffee + September dinner)

If the window closes without this effort, the market has NOT spoken — the conclusion is about commitment, not viability, and the honest next step is winding down by choice rather than blaming the idea.

## What Counts as a "Paying Customer"

ALL of the following:
1. An in-segment professional — an independent CFP/RIA (or attorney) — not a friend, relative, or pity purchase
2. Paid ≥ $99 of their own or their firm's money
3. For a real client engagement that was actually delivered (interview done, letters mailed to heirs)

A friendly pilot converts to "paying customer" only when real money changes hands at full price.

## Decision Rules (locked in advance)

### 0 paying customers → FAIL. Shut down.
Provided Minimum Valid Effort was met. Stop all spend, do not renew the TN entity next year, archive the code and domain, write a one-page postmortem. Leave with a clean conscience: the idea was killed by evidence, not exhaustion. The engineering capability gained is permanent and comes along to whatever is next.

### 1 paying customer → INTERROGATE, then extend or fail. Default is FAIL.
One sale is ambiguous — it can be signal or noise. It earns a **single 45-day extension** only if ALL three hold:
1. **Clean sale:** meets the full "paying customer" definition (stranger, full price, delivered).
2. **Evidence of pull:** the customer either re-orders for a second client, refers a peer, or gives an unprompted strong testimonial within 30 days of delivery.
3. **Live pipeline:** at least 3 additional in-segment prospects in active conversation on decision day.

If any of the three is missing, treat 1 as 0. One clean sale with no pull and no pipeline is a fluke, not a business. The extension exists to answer one question: *does customer #1 replicate?* If the extension ends without customer #2, it is a FAIL — no second extension.

### 2 paying customers → CONDITIONAL PASS. Extend 30 days to confirm.
Two independent strangers paying is unlikely to be luck. Extend 30 days with one goal: customer #3 from the existing pipeline. Meanwhile, begin acting like it's real: raise the price on the next 5 deals (test $149–199), and start the branded heir-capture page build. If #3 closes → full PASS. If not → hard review of the two accounts' economics and repeatability before any further investment.

### 3+ paying customers → PASS. It's a business.
The thesis is validated: advisors/attorneys pay for heir-acquisition letters. Next moves, in order:
1. Ask all three: "what almost stopped you from buying?" — that's the real pitch.
2. Raise price on new deals ($149–199) until someone flinches.
3. Build the firm-branded heir-capture page (QR → "Preserved by [Firm]" → heir leaves contact → advisor notified) — the acquisition claim becomes demonstrable.
4. Finish the migration cutover (Phase 2–5) so the product is fast and reliable under real usage.
5. Decide deliberately about depth vs. breadth: more Nashville attorneys vs. a second city vs. the NEC network.

## Tracking

Keep a simple log (spreadsheet or table in this repo): date, name, firm, segment, letter sent, call made, outcome, objection verbatim, next step. The objections list is a deliverable in itself — it is either the sales playbook (pass) or the postmortem (fail).

## The Point

This document exists so that failure, if it comes, is an *experiment result* — narrow, factual, defined in advance — and not an open-ended verdict on the founder. Every outcome here is a win over permanent maybe.
